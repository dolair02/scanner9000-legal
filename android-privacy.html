Privacy Policy

SCAN-PRO 9000 (Android) · Effective October 28, 2026

SCAN-PRO 9000 (“the app”, “we”) is a police-scanner style radio simulator for emergency-radio fans. It is published by Dolair. This policy covers the Android app and explains what the app stores, what leaves your phone, and the choices you have.

The short version

  • The app has no account and we run no server that receives your data. Nothing you do in the app is sent to us.
  • Your settings, the items you add and your subscription status are stored on your phone only.
  • The app has no ads and no advertising, analytics or tracking tools of ours. We do not sell personal information.
  • The app does not ask for your location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos or notifications.
  • To show weather alerts, radar, maps, pictures, fonts and news streams, your phone contacts outside services directly. They can see your IP address. They are listed below.
  • SCAN-PRO Elite is a subscription sold through Google Play. We never see your card or payment details.
  • Uninstalling the app removes everything it stored.

What the app is, and what it is not

SCAN-PRO 9000 is a simulator. The radio calls you hear are sample audio that is bundled inside the app. The channels, the dispatch log, the 911-style board, the aircraft and the cameras are simulated or use stock pictures and video. The app does not use a radio receiver, does not tune any real radio feed, and does not listen to or record anything through your microphone.

What the app stores on your device

As you use the app, it saves the following in its private storage on your phone:

  • Your settings and choices, such as the color theme, the notices you have accepted, and whether you have finished the welcome screens.
  • Your subscription status: whether Google Play says you are subscribed, so the app can open without asking every time.
  • Access code and owner sign-in status. If you enter an access code, or the owner sign-in that is meant for the publisher of the app, the app checks it on your phone against a stored scrambled (hashed) value and remembers that it is unlocked. Nothing you type there is sent to us or to anyone else.
  • Items you add yourself, such as incidents on the 911-style board and news items. A news item can be a video link you paste or an audio or video file you pick on your phone. A file you pick is played inside the app and is not uploaded anywhere.
  • The dispatch log: the simulated calls the app plays while you listen.

We do not receive any of this. It is not uploaded to us, and we cannot see, recover or delete it for you. The app is set not to take part in Android’s automatic backups, so this data is not copied to your Google account and may not move to a new phone with you.

Outside services your phone contacts

Parts of the app show real information from other organizations. When you use those parts, your phone asks the service directly. The service receives your phone’s IP address and the details of the request (what was asked for, the time, and the app and device type that your phone reports). We do not receive this information. The app does not know your location and does not send it, but an IP address can show roughly where your internet connection is. All of these requests use encrypted (HTTPS) connections.

  • National Weather Service (api.weather.gov): the weather alerts shown in the app.
  • NOAA weather radar pictures, delivered by the Iowa Environmental Mesonet at Iowa State University (mesonet.agron.iastate.edu): the radar layer on the map.
  • OpenStreetMap (tile.openstreetmap.org): the map pictures under the radar. The pieces of the map your phone asks for show the part of the map on your screen, not where you are. OpenStreetMap handles this under the OpenStreetMap Foundation privacy policy. Map data is © OpenStreetMap contributors.
  • Unsplash (images.unsplash.com): some of the pictures shown in the app.
  • YouTube (youtube.com and youtube-nocookie.com): the live news streams. A stream’s player is loaded from YouTube when you open it. YouTube and Google may collect information, such as device and cookie identifiers, under Google’s Privacy Policy. A “watch on YouTube” link opens the same video in your browser or in the YouTube app.
  • Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com): the text fonts, which are loaded when the app starts.

Each of these services has its own privacy policy. If you do not want a service to see your IP address, do not use the part of the app that needs it.

Copying and sharing

Copy Intel copies the text of the current call caption to your clipboard. Share Ch opens Android’s share sheet, or copies a short text about a channel. Nothing is sent anywhere until you choose an app and send it. Other apps, such as keyboards and clipboard managers, may be able to see what is on the clipboard under Android’s rules. We never receive what you copy or share.

Subscriptions and Google Play

The app uses the Google Play Billing Library to offer the SCAN-PRO Elite subscription. When you start, restore or manage a subscription, the app talks to Google Play, which processes the purchase and tells the app whether your subscription is active. Payment is handled by Google under Google’s Privacy Policy; we never see or store your payment details.

When the app’s Google Play components communicate with Google, Google may collect limited technical information, such as device and app information and device or other identifiers, as described in Google’s Privacy Policy. We do not receive it, and we do not use it for advertising.

Permissions

SCAN-PRO 9000 asks for these Android permissions:

  • Internet and network status, to load weather alerts, radar, maps, pictures, fonts and news streams, and to talk to Google Play.
  • Google Play Billing, for the subscription.

It does not ask for location, camera, microphone, contacts, calendar, SMS, phone, storage or notification permissions. If you add a news item from a file, Android’s own file picker opens and the app receives only the file you choose.

Links to other websites

Some screens link to web pages, such as this policy, the terms of use, the support page, YouTube and Google Play’s subscription settings. They open in your browser or in another app, and the privacy policy of that site applies.

Security

The data you enter is kept in the app’s private storage, which other apps cannot read on a standard, unmodified Android device. Communication with the outside services above and with Google Play is encrypted in transit. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure.

Retention and deletion

Your data stays on your phone until you remove it. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android settings, removes everything the app stores on your phone. Because we do not hold your data on any server, there is nothing for us to delete on our side.

Your choices and rights

You control what the app stores. You can manage or cancel your subscription in Google Play (profile picture, then Payments & subscriptions, then Subscriptions). Depending on where you live (for example the EEA, the UK or California), you may have additional rights over personal information held about you. Since we hold none of your data, such requests mostly concern your contact with us; email us and we will respond within 30 days.

Children

The app is intended for adults and is not directed to children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will update the effective date above. If a change is material, we will also tell you in the app where required.
